# Pure Mathematics 2: Exponentials and Logarithms

## 1. Exponential Functions
An exponential function has the form $f(x) = a^x$ where $a > 0$ and $a \neq 1$.
* The graph of $y = a^x$ always passes through $(0,1)$.
* As $x \to -\infty$, $y \to 0$ (horizontal asymptote at $y=0$) if $a > 1$.
* The inverse of an exponential function is a logarithmic function.

## 2. Laws of Logarithms
By definition, $y = a^x \iff x = \log_a(y)$, where $a$ is the base, $a > 0$, $y > 0$.

### Core Identities
* $\log_a(a) = 1$
* $\log_a(1) = 0$

### Fundamental Laws
* Product Law: $\log_a(xy) = \log_a(x) + \log_a(y)$
* Quotient Law: $\log_a\left(\frac{x}{y}\right) = \log_a(x) - \log_a(y)$
* Power Law: $\log_a(x^k) = k\log_a(x)$

### Change of Base Formula
$$\log_a(x) = \frac{\log_b(x)}{\log_b(a)}$$
This is crucial for evaluating logarithms on standard bases such as base 10 (common log) or base $e$ (natural log).

## 3. Solving Exponential Equations
When solving equations where the unknown is in the exponent (e.g., $a^x = b$):

### Single Base Equations
1. Take logarithms of both sides (typically base 10 or natural log).
2. Apply the power law to bring down the exponent: $x \log(a) = \log(b)$.
3. Rearrange to isolate the variable: $x = \frac{\log(b)}{\log(a)}$.

### Hidden Quadratics
Equations of the form $A(a^{2x}) + B(a^x) + C = 0$ must be simplified using substitution:
1. Let $u = a^x$.
2. Rewrite the equation as a standard quadratic: $Au^2 + Bu + C = 0$.
3. Solve for $u$.
4. Re-substitute $a^x = u$ to solve for $x$. Discard any values of $u \le 0$ since $a^x > 0$ for all real $x$.

## 4. Exponential Growth and Decay
Real-world systems modeled by $V = V_0 k^t$ or $V = V_0 a^{-t}$ can be analyzed linearly using logarithms.

### Linearization Transformation
Given a non-linear relationship $y = ax^n$:
$$\log(y) = \log(ax^n) \implies \log(y) = \log(a) + n\log(x)$$
Plotting $\log(y)$ against $\log(x)$ generates a straight line with:
* $\text{Gradient} = n$
* $\text{Vertical Intercept} = \log(a)$

Given an exponential relationship $y = ab^x$:
$$\log(y) = \log(ab^x) \implies \log(y) = \log(a) + x\log(b)$$
Plotting $\log(y)$ against $x$ generates a straight line with:
* $\text{Gradient} = \log(b)$
* $\text{Vertical Intercept} = \log(a)$
