# Pure Mathematics 2: Algebraic Methods

## 1. Algebraic Fractions
Simplifying algebraic fractions requires factoring polynomials completely and cancelling common linear or quadratic factors. 

### Multiplication and Division
To multiply fractions, multiply numerators together and denominators together. To divide, multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor. Always factorise prior to expansion to identify cancellations early.
$$\frac{a^2 - b^2}{a^2 + 2ab + b^2} \times \frac{a+b}{a-b} = \frac{(a-b)(a+b)}{(a+b)^2} \times \frac{a+b}{a-b} = 1$$

### Addition and Subtraction
To add or subtract algebraic fractions, find the Lowest Common Denominator (LCD). Express each fraction with the LCD before combining numerators.
$$\frac{3}{x-2} - \frac{2}{x+1} = \frac{3(x+1) - 2(x-2)}{(x-2)(x+1)} = \frac{3x + 3 - 2x + 4}{(x-2)(x+1)} = \frac{x+7}{(x-2)(x+1)}$$

## 2. Polynomial Division
An improper algebraic fraction is one where the degree of the numerator $P(x)$ is greater than or equal to the degree of the denominator $Q(x)$. Improper fractions must be converted into a polynomial and a proper fraction using long division or the method of equating coefficients.

$$\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} = F(x) + \frac{R(x)}{Q(x)}$$
Where $F(x)$ is the quotient and $R(x)$ is the remainder, with $\text{deg}(R) < \text{deg}(Q)$.

### Long Division Algorithm
1. Arrange terms in descending order of powers. Include zero coefficients for missing powers.
2. Divide the leading term of the dividend by the leading term of the divisor to get the first term of the quotient.
3. Multiply the divisor by this new term and subtract from the dividend.
4. Repeat the process with the remainder until the degree of the remaining polynomial is strictly less than the divisor.

## 3. The Factor Theorem
For a polynomial $f(x)$, if $f(a) = 0$, then $(x-a)$ is a linear factor of $f(x)$. Conversely, if $(x-a)$ is a factor of $f(x)$, then $f(a) = 0$.
* General Case: If $f\left(\frac{b}{a}\right) = 0$, then $(ax-b)$ is a factor of $f(x)$.

### Applications
* Fully factorising cubic and quartic polynomials.
* Finding unknown constants within polynomials given their factor structural properties.
* Solving higher-degree polynomial equations after reducing them via long division.

## 4. The Remainder Theorem
When a polynomial $f(x)$ is divided by a linear expression $(ax - b)$, the constant remainder $R$ is given by evaluating the polynomial at $x = \frac{b}{a}$.
$$f(x) = (ax - b)Q(x) + R \implies f\left(\frac{b}{a}\right) = 0 \cdot Q\left(\frac{b}{a}\right) + R = R$$

### Step-by-Step Solving Protocol
If $f(x)$ leaves a remainder $R_1$ when divided by $(x-a)$ and a remainder $R_2$ when divided by $(x-b)$:
1. Set up the equation $f(a) = R_1$.
2. Set up the equation $f(b) = R_2$.
3. Solve the resulting system of simultaneous equations to determine coefficients.

## 5. Mathematical Proof
### Proof by Exhaustion
Demonstrating that a statement holds true by testing every possible case or breaking the domain down into a finite number of mutually exclusive cases (e.g., testing odd and even integers separately).

### Proof by Counter-Example
Disproving a general mathematical statements by identifying a single specific instance where the statement fails to hold. A valid counter-example must satisfy the hypothesis of the statement but contradict its conclusion.
