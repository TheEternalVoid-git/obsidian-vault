# Pure Mathematics 2: Trigonometry

## 1. Radian Measure
A radian is the angle subtended at the centre of a circle by an arc equal in length to the radius.
$$\pi \text{ radians} = 180^\circ \implies 1 \text{ radian} = \frac{180^\circ}{\pi}$$

### Arc Length and Sector Area Formulas
When the angle $\theta$ is measured in radians:
* Arc Length: 
  $$s = r\theta$$
* Area of a Sector: 
  $$A = \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$$
* Area of a Segment: 
  $$A_{\text{segment}} = A_{\text{sector}} - A_{\text{triangle}} = \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta - \frac{1}{2}r^2\sin\theta = \frac{1}{2}r^2(\theta - \sin\theta)$$

## 2. Trigonometric Identities
Two core identities must be retained for simplifying terms and transforming equations:
1. $\tan\theta \equiv \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} \quad (\cos\theta \neq 0)$
2. $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta \equiv 1$
   * $\sin^2\theta \equiv 1 - \cos^2\theta$
   * \$\cos^2\theta \equiv 1 - \sin^2\theta$

## 3. Trigonometric Equations
To solve trigonometric equations systematically across specified intervals:

### Exact Values to Memorise
| $\theta$ (deg) | $\theta$ (rad) | $\sin\theta$ | $\cos\theta$ | $\tan\theta$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $0^\circ$ | $0$ | $0$ | $1$ | $0$ |
| $30^\circ$ | $\frac{\pi}{6}$ | $\frac{1}{2}$ | $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ | $\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ |
| $45^\circ$ | $\frac{\pi}{4}$ | $\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ | $\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ | $1$ |
| $60^\circ$ | $\frac{\pi}{3}$ | $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ | $\frac{1}{2}$ | $\sqrt{3}$ |
| $90^\circ$ | $\frac{\pi}{2}$ | $1$ | $0$ | $\text{Undefined}$ |

### Solution Strategy Protocol
1. Isolate the trigonometric function. If multiple functions exist, use identities to transform the equation into a single function (e.g., replacing $\sin^2 x$ with $1 - \cos^2 x$ to form a quadratic in $\cos x$).
2. Compute the principal value (PV) using the inverse function: $\theta_1 = \sin^{-1}(k)$.
3. Use quadrant rules (CAST diagram) or symmetries of the graph to determine secondary values within the required interval:
   * For Sine: $\theta_2 = 180^\circ - \theta_1$ (or $\pi - \theta_1$)
   * For Cosine: $\theta_2 = 360^\circ - \theta_1$ (or $2\pi - \theta_1$)
   * For Tangent: $\theta_2 = 180^\circ + \theta_1$ (or $\pi + \theta_1$)
4. If the argument is modified (e.g., $\sin(2x + 30^\circ)$), transform the boundaries of the interval first, solve for the compound angle, and convert back to $x$ as the final step.
