# 04. Statics, Coplanar Forces & Friction

Statics focuses on bodies that are either completely at rest or moving at a constant velocity. In both states, the net acceleration of the system is zero.

## 1. Conditions for Static Equilibrium
For a particle to remain in static equilibrium under a system of coplanar forces, the vector sum of all forces must equal zero. When resolved into orthogonal component axes, this yields two independent scalar criteria:
$$\Sigma F_x = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \Sigma F_y = 0$$

If forces are non-orthogonal, resolve them using basic trigonometry before applying the equilibrium equations:
* Horizontal component: $F_x = F \cos(\theta)$
* Vertical component: $F_y = F \sin(\theta)$

## 2. Coulomb's Model of Dry Friction
Friction is a reactive contact force that acts parallel to the interface between surfaces, directly opposing any relative tangential displacement or intended motion.

### The Mathematical Mechanics
The magnitude of the friction force $F$ satisfies the following condition:
$$F \le \mu R$$

Where:
* $\mu$: The scalar coefficient of friction (dependent purely on the material properties of the contact interfaces).
* $R$: The Normal Reaction force acting perpendicular to the contact plane.

### The Three Operational Phases of Friction
1. **Static Phase (No Motion):** The applied force is low. Friction matches the applied force exactly to maintain equilibrium ($F < \mu R$).
2. **Limiting Equilibrium Phase:** The body is completely stationary but is **on the verge of slipping**. Friction has reached its absolute capacity:
   $$F = F_{\text{max}} = \mu R$$
3. **Kinetic/Dynamic Phase (In Motion):** The applied force breaks equilibrium. The body slides, and the friction force remains locked at its maximum value:
   $$F = \mu R$$

## 3. Resolving Forces on Ramped / Inclined Planes
For problems involving sloped surfaces, it is highly recommended to tilt your coordinate axes. Align the x-axis **parallel** to the plane surface and the y-axis **perpendicular** to it.

![[Inclined plane forces.png|692]]
### Component Deconstruction of Weight ($mg$)
The weight force acts vertically straight down towards the center of the earth. When mapped to the tilted coordinate frame, it splits into two perpendicular vector components:
* **Component Parallel to the Incline:** Pulls the object down the ramp.
  $$W_{\parallel} = mg \sin(\theta)$$
* **Component Perpendicular to the Incline:** Presses the object directly into the ramp surface.
  $$W_{\perp} = mg \cos(\theta)$$

### Setting up Equilibrium Equations on an Incline
If a block of mass $m$ sits in limiting equilibrium on a rough plane inclined at an angle $\theta$, with a force pulling it up the slope:
* **Perpendicular Equilibrium ($\Sigma F_y = 0$):**
  $$R - mg \cos(\theta) = 0 \implies R = mg \cos(\theta)$$
* **Parallel Equilibrium ($\Sigma F_x = 0$):**
  $$\Sigma F_{\text{up}} = \Sigma F_{\text{down}} \implies F_{\text{pull}} = F_{\text{friction}} + mg \sin(\theta)$$
