# 03. Dynamics & Newton's Laws of Motion

Dynamics links the acceleration of a body to the forces acting upon it using its mass as the property of inertia.

## 1. Newton's Three Laws of Motion
* **First Law (Inertia):** A body remains at rest or continues at a constant velocity unless acted upon by a net external resultant force.
* **Second Law (Fundamental Law of Dynamics):** The net resultant force acting on a body is directly proportional to, and acts in the same direction as, its acceleration vector.
  $$\Sigma \mathbf{F} = m\mathbf{a}$$
* **Third Law (Action-Reaction):** When body A exerts a force on body B, body B simultaneously exerts a force of equal magnitude and opposite direction on body A ($F_{\text{AB}} = -F_{\text{BA}}$).

## 2. Comprehensive Connected Bodies Workflows
When systems contain multiple masses linked mechanically, you can analyze them using two methods: **Isolated Body Analysis** or **Combined System Analysis**.

### Strategy A: Isolated Body Analysis (Recommended)
1. Isolate every single mass in the system.
2. Draw a clean **Free Body Diagram (FBD)** for each object showing only the forces acting directly on it.
3. Establish the direction of acceleration for each body and define that direction as positive.
4. Formulate $\Sigma F = ma$ for each object independently.
5. Solve the resulting system of simultaneous linear equations.

### Strategy B: Combined System Analysis
* Useful only when all components share the exact same linear acceleration vector.
* You can treat the entire setup as one giant mass ($M_{\text{total}} = \sum m_i$). Internal forces (like internal string tensions) cancel out, leaving:
  $$\Sigma F_{\text{external}} = M_{\text{total}} \cdot a$$

## 3. Classic Examination Scenarios

### A. The Classic Pulley Setup (Atwood Machine)
Two masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ ($m_1 > m_2$) are suspended over a light, smooth fixed pulley via a light, inextensible string.
![[Atwood Machine.png|636]]
* **For Mass 1 (Moving downwards):**
  $$m_1 g - T = m_1 a \quad \text{--- (Eq 1)}$$
* **For Mass 2 (Moving upwards):**
  $$T - m_2 g = m_2 a \quad \text{--- (Eq 2)}$$
* **Solving for Acceleration ($a$):** Add (Eq 1) and (Eq 2) to eliminate $T$:
  $$(m_1 - m_2)g = (m_1 + m_2)a \implies a = \left(\frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\right)g$$

### B. Lift Mechanics & Apparent Weight
A passenger of mass $m$ stands on a weighing scale inside a lift of mass $M$ pulled upwards by a cable tension $T_{\text{cable}}$. The scale registers the normal contact force ($R$) acting on the passenger.

* **Scenario 1: Lift Accelerating Upwards ($+a$)**
  * Individual passenger: $R - mg = ma \implies R = m(g + a)$ (The passenger feels heavier).
* **Scenario 2: Lift Accelerating Downwards ($-a$)**
  * Individual passenger: $mg - R = ma \implies R = m(g - a)$ (The passenger feels lighter).
* **Scenario 3: Complete System Analysis**
  * Combined system equation to find cable tension: $T_{\text{cable}} - (M + m)g = (M + m)a$.
