# 04 - Differentiation

## 1. Differentiation from First Principles
The derivative represents the instantaneous rate of change / gradient of a curve at a single point.
$$\frac{\text{d}y}{	\text{d}x} = \lim_{h 	\to 0}\frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$$
* *Tip:* Be ready to expand terms like $(x+h)^3$ algebraically using expanding brackets or binomial methods before dividing by $h$.

## 2. Core Rule for Polynomials
If $y = kx^n$, then:
$$\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x} = nkx^{n-1}$$
Always rearrange expressions into clean indices forms (e.g., change $rac{3}{x^2}$ to $3x^{-2}$ and $\sqrt{x}$ to $x^{1/2}$) before attempting to differentiate.

## 3. Tangents and Normals
* **Tangent Equation:** The tangent shares the exact same gradient as the curve at that point. Find $m = \frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}$ at $x_1$, then substitute into $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$.
* **Normal Equation:** The normal line runs exactly perpendicular to the tangent surface. The normal gradient is $-\frac{1}{m}$. Use $y - y_1 = -\frac{1}{m}(x - x_1)$.

## 4. Stationary Points & Extrema
A stationary point occurs when the curve gradient flattens completely:
$$\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x} = 0$$

### Classification Test
Evaluate the second derivative at the critical coordinate point:
* $\frac{\text{d}^2y}{\text{d}x^2} > 0$: **Local Minimum**
* $\frac{\text{d}^2y}{\text{d}x^2} < 0$: **Local Maximum**
* $\frac{\text{d}^2y}{\text{d}x^2} = 0$: *Test fails.* Check gradients immediately to the left and right of the coordinate point instead.
