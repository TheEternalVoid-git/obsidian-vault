# Integration

Integration acts as the reverse operation of differentiation. It is used to find functions from their derivatives and to calculate areas under curves.

## 1. Indefinite Integration
The fundamental rule for integrating algebraic power terms:
$$\int x^n \, \text{d}x = \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} + C \quad (n \neq -1)$$

### Core Integration Principles
* **Constant Factor Rule:** $\int k f(x) \, \text{d}x = k \int f(x) \, \text{d}x$
* **Sum/Difference Rule:** $\int [f(x) \pm g(x)] \, \text{d}x = \int f(x) \, \text{d}x \pm \int g(x) \, \text{d}x$

> [!WARNING] The Constant of Integration
> Never forget to include $+ C$ for indefinite integrals. To find the specific value of $C$, substitute the coordinates of a known point $(x, y)$ that lies on the original curve back into your integrated equation.

---

## 2. Definite Integration & Area Under a Curve
A definite integral has limits of integration and calculates the exact numerical value of the area trapped between a curve, the x-axis, and vertical lines $x = a$ and $x = b$.

$$\int_{a}^{b} f'(x) \, \text{d}x = [f(x)]_{a}^{b} = f(b) - f(a)$$

### Step-by-Step Area Workflow
1. Sketch the curve to see if it crosses the x-axis within your limits.
2. Set up the integral with the lower boundary $a$ at the bottom and the upper boundary $b$ at the top.
3. Integrate the expression (leave out $+ C$, as it cancels out during subtraction).
4. Substitute the upper limit $b$ first, then subtract the substitution of the lower limit $a$.

> [!DANGER] Areas Below the x-Axis
> If a curve goes below the x-axis, the definite integral will evaluate to a **negative value**. Area cannot be negative. If you need the total area across a curve that crosses the axis, calculate the integrals for the sections above and below the x-axis **separately**, take the absolute value of the negative result, and add them together.

---

## 3. Areas Between Curves and Lines
To find the area bounded between a curve $y = f(x)$ and a straight line $y = g(x)$ where $f(x) \geq g(x)$:

$$\text{Area} = \int_{a}^{b} [f(x) - g(x)] \, \text{d}x$$

### Process:
1. Find the limits $a$ and $b$ by setting $f(x) = g(x)$ and solving for $x$ to locate the intersection points.
2. Subtract the lower equation from the upper equation.
3. Integrate the resulting expression between your intersection limits.
